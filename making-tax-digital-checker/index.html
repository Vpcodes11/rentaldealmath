What Making Tax Digital means for landlords

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax replaces the annual Self Assessment return for landlords and sole traders above the income threshold. Instead of one return a year, you keep digital records of your rental income and expenses, send a summary to HMRC every quarter, and finish the year with a final declaration by 31 January.

Each quarterly update is cumulative. It covers the tax year so far, from 6 April to the end of the quarter, and shows totals for each HMRC category rather than individual receipts. The standard periods end on 5 July, 5 October, 5 January and 5 April, with deadlines on 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May. You can choose calendar quarters ending on the last day of June, September, December and March instead.

How qualifying income is worked out

Qualifying income is your gross income from self-employment and property, before expenses, added together. UK tax residents include foreign property income. Employment income, pensions, dividends, savings interest and your share of a partnership's profits are left out. For jointly owned property only your share counts. For example, £25,000 of rent plus £27,000 of self-employment turnover gives qualifying income of £52,000.

How to get ready

Choose software or a spreadsheet plus bridging software, start recording income and expenses in HMRC's categories from the first day of your start year, and sign up through GOV.UK before your first quarterly deadline. Many landlords keep a spreadsheet and let their accountant send the updates.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as qualifying income for Making Tax Digital?

Your gross income from self-employment and property, before expenses, added together. It is taken from the Self Assessment return for the relevant tax year. Employment income, pensions, dividends, savings interest and your share of partnership profits do not count.

I own a rental property jointly. Do I count the whole rent?

No. Only your share counts. If a property earns £50,000 a year and you own half, your qualifying income from it is £25,000.

Which year decides my start date?

Income over £50,000 in 2024/25 means you start on 6 April 2026. Income over £30,000 in 2025/26 means 6 April 2027. Income over £20,000 in 2026/27 means 6 April 2028. HMRC writes to people it expects to be in scope, but it is your responsibility to check.

Can I keep using a spreadsheet?

Yes, if you link it to HMRC with bridging software that sends your quarterly updates. You still keep digital records and send updates every quarter.

Are there exemptions?

Yes. For example, people who cannot use digital tools because of age, disability or location can apply for a digital exclusion exemption. Exempt people still file a Self Assessment return. See GOV.UK for the full list.

Is this calculator tax advice?

No. It applies the thresholds published on GOV.UK to the numbers you enter. Check GOV.UK or ask an accountant about your situation.
