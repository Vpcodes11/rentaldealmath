How to compare a long-term rental with Airbnb

A short-term rental can earn far more per night than a lease, but it also costs more to run. Guests expect furnishing, Wi-Fi, utilities, toiletries and a clean home at every check-in, and nights go empty between bookings. This calculator puts both options side by side on the same property, so the costs you pay either way, like the mortgage, property tax, insurance and repairs, cancel out and the real difference stands out.

Start with the shared monthly costs. Then enter the long-term rent you could realistically get, a vacancy allowance for gaps between tenants, and any management fee. For the short-term side, enter the average nightly rate across the whole year, not just peak season, and the share of nights you expect to book. Average stay length matters because every booking means another cleaning.

How the numbers are calculated

Long-term profit is twelve months of rent, reduced by vacancy and management, minus maintenance and the shared costs, divided back to a monthly figure. Short-term income is booked nights times the nightly rate, plus the cleaning fee guests pay for each stay. Booked nights are 365 times occupancy, and the number of stays is booked nights divided by the average stay. Platform and management percentages are taken from that income, then cleaning costs, utilities, maintenance and shared costs are subtracted.

Break-even occupancy is the occupancy at which both options earn the same. Above it, short-term renting wins. Below it, the lease wins. Furnishing payback divides the one-off setup cost by the extra monthly profit from short-term renting.

Worked example

With the default numbers, a $2,600 lease with 5% vacancy and 8% management leaves about $22 a month after $2,250 of shared costs and maintenance. The same home at $185 a night and 62% occupancy books about 226 nights a year across about 75 stays. After fees, cleaning and $350 a month of utilities, it clears roughly $860 a month, so short-term wins by about $840 a month and the $15,000 furnishing cost pays back in about 18 months. Break-even occupancy comes out near 47%, so a few bad months could flip the answer.

What to check before switching to short-term

Look up local short-term rental rules, permits and lodging taxes, and read your HOA rules and mortgage terms. Price seasonality honestly: a high summer rate with a weak winter can average lower than you expect. Budget for your own time too. Without a co-host, guest messages, restocking and reviews are a part-time job. If the gap between the two options is small, the steady lease is often the better risk-adjusted choice. Frequently asked questions

What occupancy rate should I use for an Airbnb?

Use real numbers for comparable listings in the same neighborhood, size and season. Many markets average between 50% and 70% over a full year, but it varies widely. The break-even occupancy shown in the results tells you the lowest occupancy at which short-term renting still beats a long-term lease.

Does the calculator include furnishing costs?

Yes. Enter what it costs to furnish and equip the property. The calculator shows how many months of extra short-term profit it takes to pay that back. It is a one-off cost, so it is not deducted from monthly cash flow.

Why is cleaning counted as both income and a cost?

Guests usually pay a cleaning fee per stay, which is income. You or a cleaner then pay for each turnover, which is a cost. Short average stays mean more turnovers, so both numbers rise together.

Is short-term renting allowed everywhere?

No. Many cities restrict or license short-term rentals, and some HOAs and lenders ban them. Check local rules, permits and occupancy taxes before you commit.

Are taxes included?

Shared costs such as mortgage, property tax and insurance can be entered. Income tax and local occupancy or lodging taxes are not modeled, so add them separately if they apply to you.
