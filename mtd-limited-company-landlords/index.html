Do limited company landlords need Making Tax Digital for Income Tax?

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax is for individuals. A limited company pays Corporation Tax, not Income Tax, so its rental income is outside these rules.

Company-owned property

If your buy-to-lets are owned by a limited company, that company's rent does not count towards your qualifying income and the company does not send MTD Income Tax updates. The company keeps filing its accounts and Corporation Tax return as usual.

Your salary and dividends from the company

Money you take out of the company as salary or dividends is not qualifying income either. So a landlord whose whole portfolio sits in a company is usually not in scope.

Property you also own personally

If you own some property in your own name as well, that rent does count. You are in scope if your personal rent plus any self-employment turnover is over the threshold, even if the company owns most of the portfolio.

Partnerships

Your share of partnership profits is not qualifying income for now, and partnerships are not part of the current timetable. This is different from simple joint ownership, which is not a partnership for tax in most cases.

Thinking of incorporating to avoid MTD?

Moving property into a company has big tax costs of its own, such as capital gains tax and stamp duty land tax. Making Tax Digital alone is rarely a good reason. Speak to an accountant before you decide.

Frequently asked questions

Does MTD for Income Tax apply to my property company?

No. Companies pay Corporation Tax and are outside Making Tax Digital for Income Tax.

Do my dividends count towards the threshold?

No. Dividends and salary are not qualifying income.

I own one flat personally and five in a company. Am I in scope?

Only the personal flat counts. You are in scope only if its rent, plus any self-employment turnover, is over the threshold.

Are partnerships included?

Not under the current timetable. Your share of partnership profit is left out of qualifying income.
