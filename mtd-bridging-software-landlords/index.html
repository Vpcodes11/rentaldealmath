Bridging software for landlords: keep your spreadsheet for Making Tax Digital

HMRC does not accept updates typed into a website. They must come from compatible software. If you already keep your rental records in a spreadsheet, bridging software is the link that sends your figures to HMRC.

What bridging software does

Bridging software reads the totals from your spreadsheet, either cell references you map once or an upload of the file, and submits them to HMRC as your quarterly update. Your spreadsheet stays the place where you record everything.

Why landlords use it

  • You keep a record you already understand.
  • It is often cheaper than full bookkeeping software.
  • Your accountant can still work from the same spreadsheet.

Digital links

HMRC requires a digital link between your records and the submission. Copying figures by hand into another program does not meet the rules. Bridging software that reads the spreadsheet directly does.

How to choose

  • Check it is on the GOV.UK list of software that works with Making Tax Digital for Income Tax.
  • Make sure it supports UK property, and foreign property if you have it.
  • Check it handles the year-end tax return too, or that your accountant has software that does.
  • Look at the price per year and whether it covers more than one income source.

Getting your spreadsheet ready

Frequently asked questions

Can I use Excel for Making Tax Digital?

Yes, for your records, as long as you send the updates with compatible bridging software that links to the spreadsheet digitally.

Does Google Sheets work?

Many bridging tools accept Google Sheets or an exported file. Check the tool before you choose it.

Where do I find compatible software?

GOV.UK publishes a list of software that works with Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, with filters for features like bridging and UK property.

Can my accountant submit for me?

Yes. An agent can sign you up and send your updates using their own software.
