HMRC property income and expense categories for Making Tax Digital

A Making Tax Digital quarterly update is not a list of receipts. It is a set of totals, one for each HMRC category. If your records are already split into these categories, the update takes minutes.

HMRC UK property categories at a glance

CategoryWhat typically goes here
Rents and other income from propertyRent received, including your share for joint property.
Premiums for the grant of a leaseOne-off premiums paid by a tenant for a lease.
Reverse premiums and inducementsPayments you receive as an incentive to take a lease.
Other property incomeFor example, payments for use of furniture, or service charges you receive.
Premises running costsGround rent, service charges, council tax and utilities you pay, buildings insurance.
Repairs and maintenanceLike-for-like repairs, redecorating between tenants, gas safety checks.
Financial costs (non-residential)Loan interest on commercial property. Residential mortgage interest goes in residential finance costs.
Professional feesLetting agent and management fees, accountancy, legal fees for renewing a lease.
Cost of services providedGardening, cleaning or wages for services to tenants.
Travel costsTravel to and between your properties for the letting business.
Other allowable property expensesPhone, stationery, advertising for tenants, replacing domestic items.
Residential property finance costsMortgage interest on residential lets. Relief is a 20% tax credit, not a deduction.

Simplified summary. Your MTD software or bridging tool shows the exact fields HMRC asks for.

Income categories

Most landlords only use one: rents and other income from property. Premiums and reverse premiums are rare and mostly apply to longer commercial leases.

Expense categories

Each cost you pay goes into one category. Repairs are the most common mistake: replacing a broken boiler with a similar one is a repair, but adding an extension is capital improvement and is not an allowable expense in the quarterly update at all.

Residential finance costs are separate

Mortgage interest on a residential buy-to-let does not reduce your rental profit. Instead you get a tax credit at the basic rate (20%). That is why it has its own category, apart from other costs.

Consolidated expenses for smaller landlords

If your property income is below the VAT registration threshold (currently £90,000), you can send one total for all expenses instead of splitting them by category. Many landlords still keep the split in their records because it makes the year-end return easier and helps spot mistakes.

Joint property

Record only your share of the income and costs. If you own a property 50/50, half of each figure goes in your update.

A tip that saves time

Frequently asked questions

Can I send one figure for all expenses?

Yes, if your property income is below the VAT threshold (currently £90,000) you can send consolidated expenses as a single total.

Where does mortgage interest go?

For residential lets, in residential property finance costs. It gives a 20% tax credit rather than reducing profit.

Is a new kitchen a repair?

Replacing an old kitchen with a similar standard is usually a repair. Upgrading or extending is usually capital and not an expense in the quarterly update.

Do I send receipts to HMRC?

No. You send category totals. You keep the underlying digital records.
