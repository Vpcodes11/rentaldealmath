What counts as qualifying income for Making Tax Digital?

Whether Making Tax Digital applies to you depends on one number: your qualifying income. It is easy to get wrong because it is not your profit and not your total income.

The rule in one line

Qualifying income is your gross income from self-employment and property, before expenses, added together. It comes from the Self Assessment return for the tax year HMRC looks at.

What counts

  • Rent from UK property, before mortgage interest, repairs, letting fees or any other cost.
  • Foreign property income, if you are UK resident.
  • Turnover from self-employment (sole trader sales, not profit).
  • For jointly owned property, only your share of the rent.

What does not count

  • Salary and other PAYE employment income.
  • Pensions, including state pension.
  • Dividends and savings interest.
  • Your share of a partnership's profits.
  • Property owned by a limited company you run.

Worked examples

Example 1. You earn £60,000 as an employee and get £24,000 rent a year. Qualifying income is £24,000, because the salary is ignored. You are under £30,000, so you are not in scope in 2027, but £24,000 is over £20,000, so check again for April 2028.

Example 2. You get £18,000 rent and run a side business with £15,000 turnover. Qualifying income is £33,000. If that is your 2025/26 figure, you start on 6 April 2027, even though each source alone is under £20,000.

Example 3. You and your spouse own a flat that lets for £40,000 a year, split 50/50. Your qualifying income from it is £20,000. Your spouse's is also £20,000.

Which tax year is used

HMRC uses the return two years before your start year. 2024/25 income decides April 2026 (over £50,000), 2025/26 decides April 2027 (over £30,000), and 2026/27 decides April 2028 (over £20,000). If your income falls under the threshold for three years in a row, you can leave.

Frequently asked questions

Is qualifying income my profit?

No. It is gross income before expenses. A landlord with £35,000 rent and £20,000 of costs has qualifying income of £35,000, not £15,000.

Does my salary count?

No. Employment income, pensions, dividends and savings interest are left out.

Do I count all the rent on a jointly owned property?

No, only your share. Married couples and civil partners are usually treated as 50/50 unless they have told HMRC otherwise with a declaration of beneficial interests.

Does foreign rental income count?

Yes, if you are UK resident. Foreign property income is added to UK property and self-employment income.
